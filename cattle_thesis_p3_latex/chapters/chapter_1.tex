\section{Background}

Cattle monitoring requires us to understand the state of the animal, its behavior and how they change with time. A cow may eat less, rest differently or gradually lose body condition. To be able to fully grasp these changes the observations must also be linked to the particular animal. This points out the importance for indicators taken directly from the animal, and for information on housing and management in Animal welfare studies~\cite{weary2009understanding}.

Body Condition Scoring (BCS), behavior detection, and individual identification are pertinent categories of information. According to the BCS, the body's reserves are estimated by the visual aspect and quantity of tissue that covers critical areas of the body. Rather than solely relying on body dimensions, scoring systems typically evaluate regions such as the tailhead, vertebrae, loin, and pelvis~\cite{edmonson1989bcs,ferguson1994bcs}. Behavior recognition encompasses actions such as eating, drinking, standing, lying down, and walking. Repeated observations are associated with the same animal through an individual identification. When combined, these tasks can provide a more comprehensive understanding of an animal than any single activity.

This is a time consuming process requiring a certain level of expertise to gather this information manually. Short observation periods can easily overlook routine welfare events. Sensors and data processing are used in precision livestock farming to improve the frequency and continuity of monitoring. Sani et al.~\cite{sani2026ab25} and Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026ab26} discuss the difficulty of converting the obtained measurements into usable information for farm decision making. This requires that the observations are reliable and linked to the right animal over time.

Computer vision provides a useful way to observe appearance, posture, and movement without attaching a separate sensor to every animal. Reviews of vision-based livestock monitoring describe many applications in dairy farming and other livestock settings, but they also report problems when models are used under changing or less controlled conditions~\cite{antognoli2025ani1,zin2026ab26}. Therefore, the value of computer vision is not only that it can automate one task. It can also provide several related measurements from the same image or video source.

Deep learning allows these visual features to be learned directly from data. Reusable image encoders such as ResNet and EfficientNet reduce the need to design a separate feature-extraction method for every task~\cite{he2016resnet,efficientnet2019}. Previous cattle studies have used learned features for body condition assessment, behavior recognition, and individual matching. However, combining these tasks is more difficult because each task may need different visual information.

Multi-task learning (MTL) trains several tasks with some shared model components and separate task outputs. Sharing can help tasks reuse useful features, but it can also hurt performance if the shared features are not suitable for every task. Research on task grouping and selective sharing therefore treats the relationship between tasks as something that should be tested rather than assumed~\cite{standley2020tasks,misra2016crossstitch}. This thesis studies that problem for cattle body condition, behavior, and identity.

\section{Rationale of the Study and Motivation}

\subsection{Task-Appropriate Visual Representations}

A common MTL design uses one image encoder and places several prediction heads after it. This is simple and efficient, but the fact that several tasks use the same image does not mean that they need the same visual information. Two tasks may look at the same cow while focusing on very different features. This study is motivated by the need to handle those differences inside one framework.

BCS mainly depends on body shape and visible condition-related anatomy. Behavior recognition depends on posture, movement, and sometimes the nearby environment. Re-identification (Re-ID) depends on stable differences between cows, such as coat markings and body shape. Because of this, a feature that helps one task may distract another. Coat pattern is useful for telling two cows apart, but it should not become a shortcut for predicting body condition. A feeding area may help explain an activity, but a Re-ID model should not recognize a cow only because it usually appears in one location.

This problem is associated with shortcut learning. Shortcut learning is when a model learns an easy correlation, rather than the relationship we actually want it to learn. This problem is described by Geirhos et al.~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} in deep learning and image backgrounds can strongly affect recognition as shown by Xiao et al.~\cite{xiao2021background}. The cow and its surrounding environment are present in farm images, so it is important to understand what information the model uses, especially if the camera, farm or recording condition is changed.

In this thesis, \emph{cattle-centred representation learning} is defined as the organization of the visual input around the animal and the task. Localization finds the target cow. Segmentation isolates the visible cow from the rest of the scene. Body structure can be described using pose or anatomical information, and video can be used to show changes in posture and movement. These cues are seen as possibilities to try, not features that are automatically assumed to help every task. The idea is to figure out what can be shared, what should stay task-specific, and when surrounding context is still helpful.

\subsection{Preliminary Investigations and Their Limitations}

A standard pre-trained visual backbone with multiple prediction heads was implemented during the initial phases of the investigation. The multitask configuration described above performed worse than the single-task configuration in terms of bodily state and behavior. This implied that merely increasing the number of outputs of a single shared network was insufficient. This prompted us to meticulously examine the information that each step necessitated.

Data review showed that different images are not necessarily independent. Some of the images may be from the same passage of a cow, adjacent moments of the same video, or synchronized perspectives of a single event. A passage label or temporary tracking ID is not necessarily a true biological cow ID either. These details affect how the train--test split evaluates generalization and helps prevent data leakage. Thus, the study groups the observations using the identity and recording information that is actually available in each dataset.

Adding more model components also does not automatically solve these problems. A component may improve localization but still fail to improve the final task. A representation may preserve posture but remove appearance that is useful for identity matching. For this reason, we use single-task models as reference points before studying how the tasks should be combined in one framework.

\subsection{Rationale for Jointly Studying the Three Tasks}

The three tasks are related in practice, but they need different visual information. BCS focuses on morphology, behavior recognition focuses on activity, and Re-ID focuses on individual appearance. Their connection makes integration useful, while their differences make the amount of sharing an important research question.

These tasks also react differently to change. Identity features should remain useful even when the cow changes activity, while behavior features must change when the activity changes. This is different from combining two very similar outputs, such as disease type and disease severity. Studying BCS, behavior, and Re-ID together allows us to examine where shared features help and where task-specific processing is needed.

Pretrained perception models make these comparisons possible without training every supporting model from the beginning. Promptable segmentation and transferable animal pose estimation can provide foreground and structural information~\cite{kirillov2023sam,ye2024superanimal}. However, simply using these tools is not the contribution of the thesis. The contribution comes from testing how task-appropriate cattle-centered information affects the three tasks and how that information can be combined.

\section{Problem Statement}

This thesis investigates learning and sharing of visual representations for cattle BCS and behavior recognition and Re-ID without compromising information required by each task. Generic RGB features are a good starting point, but they can also confound useful animal information with background or environmental correlations. Another problem with heavy parameter sharing is that a shared representation must support tasks that may prefer different features.

For BCS, the model ought to acquire knowledge regarding body-condition data, rather than attributes that distinctly recognize the cow or its environment. The model requires spatial and temporal data without the necessity of associating a specific action with a particular place for behavior identification. In Re-ID, the model is expected to preserve an individual's identification regardless of variations in camera perspective, backdrop, or temporal context. Consequently, satisfactory execution of a single task does not suffice to demonstrate that the same representation is applicable to all three jobs.

Previous works on anatomy-based BCS, temporal behavior recognition, metric-learning Re-ID, and selective MTL provide different pieces of the solution. The study integrates these concepts, exploring how cattle-centered information changes each task, the appropriate level of information sharing, and the models' performance under varying recording conditions.

The primary research question is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Are cattle-centric visual representations better than generic shared RGB representations in improving the robustness of body condition score, behavior recognition and cow re-identification and allowing a multi-task framework with less negative transfer?}
\end{quote}
To address this issue, we must analyze not only the task performance but also the visual information provided to the model, the sharing approach and the evaluation criteria.

\section{Objectives}

The primary objective is to develop and assess a multi-task deep learning framework for BCS, behavior recognition, and cow Re-ID using task-specific cattle-centric visual representations.

The first goal is to create evaluation protocols that preserve the relationships within each dataset. When reliable biological IDs are available, biological cow IDs are used. When they are not available, recording, passage, or session groups are used. This provides a safer basis for comparing models.

The secondary goal is to produce RGB single-task baseline models for the three tasks. These models provide a basis for assessment of the effect of changes in representation, and for the characterization of positive or negative transfer after integration.

The third objective is to study cattle-centered visual information, such as localization, foreground masks, anatomical or pose cues, and viewpoint information. Temporal information is also considered for behavior recognition. The goal is to determine which combinations are useful for each task while keeping the system practical to train and evaluate.

The fourth goal is to compare heavy sharing with a task-specific design that keeps some features or processing paths task-specific. This comparison checks to see if specialization can keep shared information useful without hurting individual tasks.

The fifth goal is to evaluate robustness to different acquisition conditions and to the existence of similar external datasets. This ties the model assessments into the larger goal of monitoring cattle outside the specific environment in which it was trained.

These objectives are expressed through three research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do cattle-centered representations affect BCS, behavior recognition, and Re-ID relative to their RGB single-task references?
    \item How can temporal information and task-specific visual cues be incorporated while preserving the different requirements of condition assessment, activity recognition, and identity matching?
    \item How does task-conditioned sharing compare with hard sharing in task performance, negative transfer, and robustness under domain change?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology in Brief}

The study uses a comparative experimental design. The data preparation stage first defines the labels and the grouping units used for evaluation. ScienceDB is the primary BCS dataset, CVB and Kaggle Beef are the main behavior sources, and SideViewCows2026 is the main Re-ID dataset. Different datasets are used because the three tasks need different types of observations and annotations.

Single-task RGB models are used as reference models. Their cattle-centered versions use localization and binary-mask guidance, while the behavior model also uses temporal aggregation. Pose, anatomy, and viewpoint cues are studied separately instead of assuming that they will transfer well across datasets. Upstream perception is checked before its output is used in a downstream model.

The multi-task investigation evaluates alternative integration strategies against matched single-task cattle-centered reference models on identical held-out test sets. A monolithic hard-shared multi-task model evaluates full parameter sharing across tasks. A modular task-private multi-task model evaluates architectural specialization using dedicated residual bottleneck adapters. A gradient-projected multi-task optimization control (PCGrad) evaluates conflict-aware shared-gradient projection while keeping the hard-shared architecture and parameter count strictly matched. Task-wise held-out comparisons quantify outcome-level negative transfer across prediction error for BCS, class-sensitive metrics for behavior recognition, and open-set retrieval for cow re-identification. Compatible external datasets provide candidate protocols for future cross-environment stress testing, treated separately from primary benchmark evaluations.

\section{Scopes and Challenges}

The framework covers three visual tasks, BCS, behavior recognition and individual cow identification (Re-ID). The thesis does not cover lameness detection. RGB imagery is the common visual input. Depth and other external data are only used when their role and label compatibility are clear. The presented framework is a research contribution to the monitoring of cattle and not a replacement of the veterinary assessment.

The tasks and datasets give different type of supervision. BCS labels vary in coverage and scoring. The sort of behavior depends on the position and the activity. certain categories are present in certain data only. Re-identification requires a well defined division between training and assessment datasets, and robust bovine identity data. ScienceDB does not do biological cow-disjoint assessment, but rather burst-group-disjoint and sequence-safe evaluation. These differences restrict the assertions that may be made from each experiment.

Viewpoint, occlusion, scale, and lighting also affect what can be seen in an image. Foreground processing can remove irrelevant background, but it may also remove useful context. Pose estimation can describe body structure, but it may still miss anatomical information needed for BCS. Therefore, the study treats component selection as something that must be tested experimentally.

Computational limits also affect the design. We therefore use pretrained models, reusable preprocessing, and focused comparisons instead of making the architecture unnecessarily large. Chapter 2 reviews the related research and the research gap. Chapter 3 describes the requirements, impacts, and constraints. Chapters 4 and 5 present the methodology and experimental analysis, and Chapter 6 presents the conclusions.

The completed study combines leakage-aware evaluation, matched single-task cattle-centered comparisons, and controlled multi-task experiments. The single-task results show task-dependent benefits from cattle-centered representations, while the multi-task comparisons show outcome-level degradation under hard sharing and selective mitigation under alternative architectural and optimization strategies. These findings motivate a unified framework that preserves shared cattle information while recognizing task-specific representation and optimization requirements.
