\section{Background}

Cattle monitoring requires understanding the animal's condition, behavior, and how these change over time. A cow can eat less, rest differently, or gradually lose body condition. In order to understand these changes, it is also necessary to connect these observations to the right individual animal. This shows the importance of using animal-based indicators along with data on housing and management in animal welfare research~\cite{weary2009understanding}. Body Condition Scoring (BCS), behavior detection, and individual identification are essential kinds of information for this purpose. BCS assesses the body reserves of an animal by visually examining the amount of tissue covering important body regions. Unlike relying only on body measurements, BCS systems mostly assess particular regions, such as the tailhead, spine, loin, and pelvis~\cite{edmonson1989bcs,ferguson1994bcs}.

Behavior detection involves behaviors such as eating, drinking, standing, lying down, and walking. Animal identification allows repeated observations to be linked with the same animal. Both tasks together provide a better understanding of the animal compared to using either of them alone. The process of gathering information on the animal is a very time-consuming activity that even requires some level of expertise. Short-term observation may lead to missing important welfare events. In precision livestock farming, sensors and data analysis help to observe the animals more frequently and continuously. According to Sani et al.~\cite{sani2026ab25} and Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026ab26}, it is not an easy task to make the collected data useful for farm management decisions. For that, the observations need to be reliable and linked to the correct animal over time.

Computer vision is an effective method of monitoring the appearance, posture, and movements of the animal without attaching sensors to each animal individually. Review articles on vision-based livestock monitoring have shown multiple examples of computer vision application on dairy farms and in other livestock systems. However, they also highlight difficulties in applying the models when conditions are not the same as those during training~\cite{antognoli2025ani1,zin2026ab26}. Therefore, the importance of computer vision is not limited to automating one specific task but rather includes its capability to obtain multiple measurements from one picture or video frame. With deep learning, visual features can be extracted directly from data while reusable image encoder models such as ResNet and EfficientNet lessen the need to design a new feature extractor for each task~\cite{he2016resnet,efficientnet2019}. Earlier researches on cows have used learned visual features for body condition scoring, behavior recognition, and individual animal identification.

Combining such tasks is complicated by the fact that each of them could require different types of visual information. Multi-task learning (MTL) consists of training several tasks simultaneously, sharing some components of the neural network while maintaining separate outputs for each task. Shared components might be helpful since tasks would be able to reuse useful features, but at the same time, they could lower performance when the shared features are not appropriate for some tasks. Therefore, research related to task grouping and selective sharing recommends testing relationships between tasks rather than assuming them~\cite{standley2020tasks,misra2016crossstitch}. This thesis investigates this problem for cattle body condition, behavior, and identity.

\section{Rationale of the Study and Motivation}

\subsection{Task-Appropriate Visual Representations}

A typical MTL architecture consists of a single image encoder and multiple prediction heads. This design is simple and efficient. However, even if tasks use the same image, they could still require different types of visual information.

Two tasks might work on the same cow and yet require different kinds of visual cues. This research is driven by the need to handle such situations in one system. BCS mainly depends on the cow's body form and visual cues about body condition. Behavior recognition requires posture and movement cues along with some contextual cues about the surroundings. The re-identification task requires distinguishing features which make one cow different from another, for instance, the coat pattern and body form. Thus, a visual cue that is useful for one task may distract another task or even mislead it. For instance, the coat pattern can be helpful in discriminating between individuals; however, it should not become a shortcut for BCS estimation. Likewise, the feeding place can be useful in recognizing an action; however, a re-identification algorithm should not identify the cow only because it is normally observed in a certain place.

This problem is associated with shortcut learning. Shortcut learning is when a model learns an easy correlation, rather than the relationship we actually want it to learn. This problem is described by Geirhos et al.~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} in deep learning and image backgrounds can strongly affect recognition as shown by Xiao et al.~\cite{xiao2021background}. The cow and its surrounding environment are present in farm images, so it is important to understand what information the model uses, especially if the camera, farm or recording condition is changed.

In this thesis, learning from \emph{cattle-centred representations} pertains to arranging the visual inputs according to the animal and requirements of each task. Localization serves as a means of finding the target cow, while segmentation isolates the animal from the background. The body structure may be encoded by means of pose or anatomical information, and video allows one to track postural and movement changes. Such cues serve as possibilities for experiments and not as features that always help in performing better in all tasks. The primary goal is the search for the shared information, the specific task information, and whether context may also be helpful.

\subsection{Preliminary Investigations and Their Limitations}

During the first stages of research, a typical pretrained visual backbone with several prediction heads was applied. This multitasking system performed poorly as compared to a single tasking system when applied in body condition and behavior situations.

The conclusion drawn is that just adding outputs to a shared network is insufficient. This is why we decided to consider the information required at each step in more detail. The analysis of the data showed that some images may not be independent of each other and may correspond to the same cow passage, neighboring frames from one video, or the same event shot from different angles simultaneously. A passage label or temporary tracking ID does not necessarily correspond to the identity of the cow. All this impacts the effectiveness of generalization measurement through the train-test split and whether data leakage can be avoided. This is why the current research groups observations by the identity and recording data available in each dataset. Adding more components to the model alone cannot automatically address these issues.

A particular element may help in making the localization more effective even though it may not enhance the final task performance. While it keeps the posture data, it may discard the appearance data which is needed to match identities. Single-task models are therefore used as the benchmark before exploring how the tasks can be integrated into one framework.

\subsection{Rationale for Jointly Studying the Three Tasks}

The three tasks are indeed related, but each one is dependent on a particular type of visual information. BCS is dependent on the body morphology of the cow, behavior recognition is dependent on the activities of the cow, and Re-ID is dependent on the appearance of the cow. Due to their interrelatedness, integration is helpful, but due to their differences, the degree of sharing becomes a subject of study. Features associated with identity should continue to be useful regardless of changes in the activity of the cow, whereas behavior features should change when the activity changes.

Pretrained perception models make these comparisons possible without training every supporting model from the beginning. Promptable segmentation and transferable animal pose estimation can provide foreground and structural information~\cite{kirillov2023sam,ye2024superanimal}. However, simply using these tools is not the contribution of the thesis. The contribution comes from testing how task-appropriate cattle-centered information affects the three tasks and how that information can be combined.

\section{Problem Statement}

This thesis investigates learning and sharing of visual representations for cattle BCS and behavior recognition and Re-ID without compromising information required by each task. Generic RGB features are a good starting point, but they can also confound useful animal information with background or environmental correlations. Another problem with heavy parameter sharing is that a shared representation must support tasks that may prefer different features.

For BCS, the model ought to acquire knowledge regarding body-condition data, rather than attributes that distinctly recognize the cow or its environment. The model requires spatial and temporal data without the necessity of associating a specific action with a particular place for behavior identification. In Re-ID, the model is expected to preserve an individual's identification regardless of variations in camera perspective, backdrop, or temporal context. Consequently, satisfactory execution of a single task does not suffice to demonstrate that the same representation is applicable to all three jobs.

However, this research does not include combinations of related tasks such as disease type and disease severity. Through analysis of BCS, Behavior, and Re-ID simultaneously, it would be possible to know in what aspects similar features are needed and in what aspects it is required to process each task separately. The previous work on BCS based on anatomy, temporal Behavior recognition, metric-learning Re-ID, and selective multi-task learning gives different approaches for solving the problem. This paper combines previous work and investigates how cattle-centered visual information affects each task, how much information should be shared, and how the models perform under different recording conditions.

The primary research question is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Are cattle-centric visual representations better than generic shared RGB representations in improving the robustness of body condition score, behavior recognition and cow re-identification and allowing a multi-task framework with less negative transfer?}
\end{quote}
To address this issue, we must analyze not only the task performance but also the visual information provided to the model, the sharing approach and the evaluation criteria.

\section{Objectives}

The primary objective is to develop and assess a multi-task deep learning framework for BCS, behavior recognition, and cow Re-ID using task-specific cattle-centric visual representations.

The first goal is to create evaluation protocols that preserve the relationships within each dataset. When reliable biological IDs are available, biological cow IDs are used. When they are not available, recording, passage, or session groups are used. This provides a safer basis for comparing models.

The secondary goal is to produce RGB single-task baseline models for the three tasks. These models provide a basis for assessment of the effect of changes in representation, and for the characterization of positive or negative transfer after integration.

The third objective of this research is investigation of cattle-centered visual information such as location of the cattle, foreground masks, anatomy/pose information, and viewpoint information. Temporal information will also be included for Behavior Recognition. The goal is to find out the combination suitable for each task while keeping the system feasible for training and evaluation.

Objective four will allow us to test heavy feature sharing vs a task-specific architecture based on the segregation of specific features or processing streams to perform certain tasks. This will allow us to ascertain whether task specialization allows for maintaining the sharing of information without hampering the efficiency of the tasks performed. Objective five will allow us to test the robustness of the system under various image acquisition conditions as well as with similar external datasets.

The above objectives give rise to the following three research questions. Firstly, what is the impact of cattle-centric representation on the BCS, Behavior Recognition and Re-ID compared to the single task RGB model? Secondly, how can we incorporate the temporal and task-specific visual information into our framework?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do cattle-centered representations affect BCS, behavior recognition, and Re-ID relative to their RGB single-task references?
    \item How can temporal information and task-specific visual cues be incorporated while preserving the different requirements of condition assessment, activity recognition, and identity matching?
    \item How does task-conditioned sharing compare with hard sharing in task performance, negative transfer, and robustness under domain change?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology in Brief}

In the present research, the efficiency of task-conditioned sharing is examined in comparison to hard sharing regarding task outcomes, negative transfer, and robustness in case of changes in domain conditions. The experimental comparison design is used. First of all, the data pre-processing stage identifies the labels and the groups which will be tested. ScienceDB will serve as the primary source of the BCS dataset; CVB and Kaggle Beef datasets will be used for the Behavior dataset; and SideViewCows2026 for the Re-ID dataset. Various datasets were selected as each task is based on different observations and labels.

Single-task RGB models are used as reference models. Their cattle-centered versions use localization and binary-mask guidance, while the behavior model also uses temporal aggregation. Pose, anatomy, and viewpoint cues are studied separately instead of assuming that they will transfer well across datasets. Upstream perception is checked before its output is used in a downstream model.

During multi-task experiments, various methods of integration will be compared to the single-task cattle-centric models on the same held-out test sets. The monolithic model with hard sharing will be tested in order to estimate the performance of full parameter sharing between the tasks. A modular task-private model with separate residual bottleneck adapters will also be tested.

PCGrad is utilized in another multi-task model to test the influence of conflict resolution in the gradients. The architecture as well as the total number of parameters remain the same as the hard-shared one. Negative transfer is assessed separately for each of the tasks through using the held-out dataset. As for BCS, prediction errors are taken into account. Class-sensitive results are evaluated in case of Behavior, whereas open set retrieval is used for Re-ID. Some other compatible datasets could be tested in the future for cross-environment evaluation, however, they remain outside the main benchmark evaluation.

In the chapter 4, it discuss about model settings, training methods, experimental controls, as well as data divisions. And in the chapter 5, it discuss about the result as well as the interpretation of the result. These experiments show what information each task needs and what happens when the tasks learn together.

\section{Scopes and Challenges}

The framework covers three visual tasks, BCS, behavior recognition and individual cow identification (Re-ID). The thesis does not cover lameness detection. RGB imagery is the common visual input. Depth and other external data are only used when their role and label compatibility are clear. The presented framework is a research contribution to the monitoring of cattle and not a replacement of the veterinary assessment.

The tasks and datasets give different type of supervision. BCS labels vary in coverage and scoring. The sort of behavior depends on the position and the activity. certain categories are present in certain data only. Re-identification requires a well defined division between training and assessment datasets, and robust bovine identity data. ScienceDB does not do biological cow-disjoint assessment, but rather burst-group-disjoint and sequence-safe evaluation. These differences restrict the assertions that may be made from each experiment.

Moreover, there are some image conditions like viewpoint, occlusion, scale, and lighting that can affect the amount of information in the picture. Foreground processing could eliminate the unnecessary background; however, sometimes valuable information could be excluded from the scene as well. Pose estimation provides structural information about the body, though it may lack some of the necessary anatomical information for BCS. That is why the effectiveness of each of the components is determined experimentally rather than being presumed beforehand.

In addition to this, the structure of the research is dependent on the computational capabilities. In this case, the use of the pre-trained models and reusability of the pre-processing methods and limitation of the comparison scope are applied not to create an excessively big model. The descriptions of the previous work and the research gap are presented in Chapter 2. The implications, requirements and limitations are discussed in Chapter 3. The description of the methodology and experimental analysis are provided in Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 correspondingly. The conclusion is made in Chapter 6.

The completed research can be associated with the leakage-aware evaluation, single-task comparisons with the cattle-centered representations and the multi-task experiment with the control group. The single task experiment revealed that the effect of cattle-centered representations is different for various tasks. Hard-sharing led to a bad performance in multi-task experiments; however, other architecture and optimization techniques have helped to reduce some of these problems. The acquired results support the idea of a unified model, which will incorporate some information sharing about cattle, but taking into consideration different representation and optimization of tasks.
