\section{Summary of Findings}

This thesis investigated whether cattle-centered visual representations and multi-task learning can support unified monitoring of dairy cattle across three distinct vision tasks: body condition scoring (BCS), behavior recognition, and individual cow re-identification (Re-ID). Across all experimental investigations, rigorous provenance tracking, leakage-aware evaluation protocols, and matched-population controls were enforced to ensure reproducible and reliable empirical conclusions.

In the single-task setting, cattle-centered representations demonstrated clear empirical utility, although their benefits were task-dependent and non-uniform across metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For \textbf{Body Condition Scoring}, evaluating on the matched population of $N = 7{,}549$ ScienceDB test images where automatic detection and segmentation succeeded (93.89\% perception coverage), localized cropping and binary foreground masks reduced physical error from 0.1929 to 0.1709 Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in BCS units and increased Acc@1 within $\pm 0.25$ units from 84.95\% to 89.40\% relative to the generic RGB baseline. However, exact classification accuracy remained modest (43.57\%), and class-balanced metrics showed minor declines (balanced accuracy of 39.70\% vs.\ 40.23\%; Macro-F1 of 0.4039 vs.\ 0.4074).
    \item For \textbf{Behavior Recognition}, evaluating on $N = 780$ matched video sequences ($T = 8$ frames; 96.42\% retained coverage), combining cattle-centered crops, foreground masks, and a lightweight 1D temporal convolutional network increased balanced accuracy from 71.30\% to 74.43\%, decreased test loss from 0.5505 to 0.4430, and improved minority-class Walking F1 from 0.2174 to 0.2456 relative to the single-frame RGB baseline. However, overall accuracy decreased slightly from 88.46\% to 87.44\%, and performance redistributed across sources (improving on feedlot camera footage while declining slightly on barn CCTV surveillance).
    \item For \textbf{Cattle Re-Identification}, evaluated under SideView Protocol A across 69 unseen cattle identities, target-centered cropping guided by released ground-truth masks improved first-match retrieval across both query conditions: Barn-to-Parlor Rank-1 increased from 58.64\% to 63.90\% (mAP from 38.32\% to 40.68\%), while Snapshot-to-Parlor Rank-1 rose from 38.88\% to 62.93\% (mAP from 27.05\% to 40.42\%). This substantial gain under handheld snapshot queries indicates that target-centered isolation is particularly beneficial when handling extreme posture, angle, and illumination shifts; however, because released ground-truth masks were used, this represents an oracle condition rather than an automatic segmentation deployment pipeline.
\end{itemize}

In the multi-task setting, joint training across the three heterogeneous tasks revealed substantial cross-task tension:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{Monolithic Hard-Shared Multi-Task Control (E1)}, which forced all three tasks through a single four-channel ResNet-18 spatial backbone, resulted in outcome-level negative transfer across all three tasks relative to matched single-task references: BCS MAE degraded from 0.1709 to 0.1788; Behavior balanced accuracy dropped from 74.43\% to 67.30\% (with Walking F1 falling from 0.2456 to 0.0408); and Re-ID Barn Rank-1 fell from 63.90\% to 57.38\% (mAP falling from 40.68\% to 30.37\%).
    \item The \textbf{Modular Task-Private Multi-Task Model (E3)}, which introduced task-private residual bottleneck adapters between the shared backbone and each task head (+395,904 trainable parameters, representing a +3.32\% capacity increase over E1), failed to provide general negative-transfer mitigation. While Behavior test loss (0.4193) and barn surveillance accuracy (80.33\%) improved over E1, BCS performance degraded further (MAE 0.1916), Re-ID retrieval declined substantially (Barn Rank-1 49.08\%, mAP 28.04\%), and Walking F1 dropped to 0.0000 on the matched held-out test set.
    \item The \textbf{Gradient-Projected Hard-Shared Multi-Task Optimization Control (E4)}, which matched E1 parameter-for-parameter (11,926,706 trainable parameters) and applied PCGrad projection exclusively to shared-backbone gradients during training~\cite{yu2020pcgrad}, provided selective, metric-dependent mitigation. During E4 training, direct optimization diagnostics recorded 44,177 gradient conflict projections across 16,140 super-steps (averaging 2.737 projections per super-step), showing that opposing shared-backbone gradient directions occurred regularly across task pairs (46.5\% to 47.8\% frequency). PCGrad improved Behavior overall accuracy (86.92\%), balanced accuracy (69.15\%), Macro-F1 (0.7114), and Walking F1 (0.0909) over E1, and recovered principal BCS and Re-ID metrics relative to E3. However, E4 remained below dedicated single-task models on several primary metrics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

The empirical findings from the single-task and multi-task investigations provide direct answers to the three research questions established in Chapter~1.

\subsection{Research Question 1}
\textit{How do cattle-centered representations affect BCS, behavior recognition, and Re-ID relative to their RGB single-task references?}

Cattle-centered visual representations improved important predictive and retrieval outcomes across all three monitoring domains, but their effectiveness was task-dependent and not uniform across metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For BCS, cattle-centered cropping and foreground mask guidance reduced MAE by 0.0220 BCS units and increased Acc@1 by 4.45 percentage points on matched test samples. The improvement was strongest on ordered-error and tolerance measures, while class-balanced metrics did not improve.
    \item For Behavior Recognition, foreground-guided temporal processing redistributed performance across classes and camera sources: balanced accuracy (+3.13 percentage points), test loss (-19.5\%), and minority Walking F1 (+0.0282) improved, but overall accuracy decreased slightly (-1.02 percentage points) and barn CCTV surveillance accuracy declined, showing that temporal foreground isolation benefited specific dynamic transitions rather than uniformly elevating all metrics.
    \item For Re-ID, target-centered representation guidance produced its largest gain under extreme acquisition shifts, improving Snapshot-to-Parlor Rank-1 by 24.05 percentage points and mAP by 13.37 percentage points, while producing a more modest 5.26 percentage-point Rank-1 improvement under stationary barn surveillance.
\end{itemize}
These outcomes demonstrate that cattle-centered visual representations provide tangible utility, but they do not universally benefit all evaluation metrics or eliminate shortcut learning across tasks. Furthermore, because these comparisons evaluated combined perception pipelines (crop plus mask for BCS and Re-ID; crop, mask, and temporal convolution for Behavior), the gains cannot be attributed to segmentation alone.

\subsection{Research Question 2}
\textit{How can temporal information and task-specific visual cues be incorporated while preserving the different requirements of condition assessment, activity recognition, and identity matching?}

The experimental results support task-specific incorporation of temporal dynamics and structural cues rather than forcing heterogeneous livestock monitoring tasks into a uniform input representation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For Body Condition Scoring, static morphology is the primary biological signal. Incorporating localized bounding-box crops and foreground binary masks provided a localized foreground representation while retaining visual morphology relevant to BCS, achieving superior error proximity without requiring temporal aggregation.
    \item For Behavior Recognition, posture and motion dynamics require temporal context. The combined eight-frame foreground-guided TCN configuration improved balanced accuracy and Walking F1 relative to the matched single-frame reference.
    \item For Cow Re-Identification, biometric coat patterns and identity features require fine-grained surface spatial detail. Cropping and mask guidance preserved localized surface geometry; the oracle-mask experiments demonstrated substantial retrieval gains under unconstrained query conditions when reliable foreground isolation is available.
    \item Anatomical pose keypoints and viewpoint priors were systematically audited. Frozen SuperAnimal pose estimation exhibited high missingness (45.60\% detector failure rate on behavior sequences, deteriorating to 72.92\% failure on recumbent/lying cattle) and lacked critical rear-pelvic landmarks required for BCS; pose was consequently excluded from the primary downstream pipeline. Real-cattle viewpoint classification achieved 86.26\% accuracy on its own domain, but was not fused into primary models because cross-domain transfer to downstream farm environments remained unverified, with high camera and source shortcut risks.
\end{itemize}
Thus, preserving task-specific requirements is best accomplished by tailoring visual cues to the physical demands of each domain---spatial morphology for BCS, temporal sequence modeling for behavior, and surface biometrics for re-identification---rather than imposing a single monolithic cue across all tasks.

\subsection{Research Question 3}
\textit{How does task-conditioned sharing compare with hard sharing in task performance, negative transfer, and robustness under domain change?}

The comparative evaluation of multi-task configurations against matched single-task baselines yielded four definitive conclusions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Outcome-Level Negative Transfer under Hard Sharing}: Monolithic hard parameter sharing (E1) degraded held-out performance across all three task domains relative to matched single-task references: BCS MAE rose by +0.0079 units; Behavior balanced accuracy dropped by -7.13 percentage points (with Walking F1 falling from 0.2456 to 0.0408); and Re-ID Barn Rank-1 dropped by -6.52 percentage points (mAP falling by -10.31 percentage points). These consistent held-out degradations confirm outcome-level negative transfer when forcing disparate cattle vision tasks into a single shared ResNet-18 spatial trunk.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation of Task-Conditioned Modularity}: Incorporating task-private residual bottleneck adapters (E3) did not provide general mitigation of negative transfer. Although E3 improved Behavior test loss (0.4193 vs.\ 0.6226) and barn surveillance accuracy (80.33\% vs.\ 76.78\%) over E1, it degraded BCS MAE further to 0.1916, lowered Re-ID Barn Rank-1 to 49.08\%, and obtained Walking F1 = 0.0000 on the matched held-out test set. Furthermore, because E3 added 395,904 trainable parameters (+3.32\% capacity over E1), its performance differences cannot be attributed purely to architectural routing.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation of Gradient-Projected Optimization}: PCGrad optimization control (E4), which matched E1 parameter-for-parameter without additional capacity, directly confirmed that opposing shared-backbone gradient directions occurred regularly across task pairs during training (44,177 conflict projections across 16,140 super-steps; 2.737 projections per super-step; 46.5\% to 47.8\% pairwise conflict frequency). Projecting conflicting gradient components during optimization provided selective mitigation: Behavior overall accuracy rose to 86.92\% (+1.92 percentage points vs.\ E1), balanced accuracy rose to 69.15\% (+1.85 percentage points), Macro-F1 rose to 0.7114 (+0.0248), and Walking F1 rose to 0.0909 (vs.\ 0.0408 in E1), while recovering principal BCS and Re-ID metrics relative to E3. However, E4 did not eliminate negative transfer: dedicated single-task models remained superior on several task-specific measures (BCS MAE 0.1709 vs.\ 0.1828 E4; Behavior balanced accuracy 74.43\% vs.\ 69.15\% E4; Walking F1 0.2456 vs.\ 0.0909 E4; Re-ID Barn Rank-1 63.90\% vs.\ 54.97\% E4).
    \item \textbf{Robustness under Domain Change}: The executed multi-task models were evaluated under source and acquisition differences within the primary protocols, such as varying camera angles in SideView (Barn vs.\ Snapshot queries) and differing recording environments in Behavior (CVB barn surveillance vs.\ Kaggle Beef feedlot footage). However, these evaluations remain confined to the executed dataset protocols; the results provide evidence under source and acquisition changes within the executed protocols, but they do not establish broad cross-dataset generalization of the final multi-task framework to unseen external commercial herds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions of the Thesis}

This thesis makes six distinct, evidence-supported contributions to computer vision for precision cattle monitoring and multi-task representation learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Aware Evaluation Protocols}: Established verifiable, provenance-preserving benchmark protocols for three heterogeneous cattle monitoring domains: a burst-group-disjoint protocol for ScienceDB BCS to prevent identical-passage video frame leakage; a session- and source-disjoint protocol for CVB and Kaggle Beef Behavior recognition; and an identity-disjoint open-set retrieval protocol (SideView Protocol A) evaluating 69 held-out cows against a fixed 36,811-image parlor gallery.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Single-Task Empirical Baselines}: Provided rigorous single-task baseline comparisons between generic RGB inputs and cattle-centered representations across matched test populations, establishing that localized cropping and foreground segmentation yield substantial, task-dependent benefits on primary metrics (e.g., reducing BCS MAE from 0.1929 to 0.1709 and increasing Re-ID Snapshot Rank-1 from 38.88\% to 62.93\%).
    \item \textbf{Task-Specific Cattle Representation Strategy}: Formulated and verified a principled, evidence-led input representation pipeline tailored to the physical demands of each task: static morphology-focused crop and mask for BCS; 8-frame sequential modeling with 1D temporal convolution for behavior; and biometric surface pattern preservation for re-identification. Audited and documented empirical boundaries for anatomical pose estimation and viewpoint priors.
    \item \textbf{Systematic Multi-Task Sharing Benchmark}: Conducted a controlled multi-task evaluation comparing monolithic hard parameter sharing (E1), modular task-private residual adapters (E3), and gradient-projected optimization (E4) against matched single-task baselines on identical held-out test sets, providing an empirical benchmark for livestock multi-task vision.
    \item \textbf{Direct Gradient Conflict Diagnostics}: Documented direct empirical measurements of shared-parameter gradient interference during multi-task training under PCGrad, recording 44,177 pairwise conflict projections across 16,140 super-steps and demonstrating regular gradient opposition (46.5\% to 47.8\% frequency) between agricultural monitoring tasks.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Understanding of Multi-Task Trade-Offs}: Provided empirical evidence that multi-task learning does not universally benefit individual agricultural tasks through shared representations, demonstrating that negative transfer is outcome-level and that mitigation strategies provide selective, metric-dependent trade-offs rather than uniform performance gains.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

To preserve academic integrity and scientific claim boundaries, several methodological and empirical limitations of this work must be acknowledged:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Absence of Verified Cow IDs in ScienceDB}: The ScienceDB dataset does not provide individual cow identifiers. Although the burst-group-disjoint protocol strictly prevents near-identical frame leakage from automated passage sequences, it cannot guarantee biological cow-disjoint evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Session-Disjoint Rather than Cow-Disjoint Behavior Evaluation}: The primary Behavior dataset combines CVB barn surveillance and Kaggle Beef feedlot clips partitioned by protected video recordings and sessions rather than biologically distinct cow identities.
    \item \textbf{Confounding of Minority Walking Class}: The minority Walking behavior class occurs exclusively in the CVB barn surveillance source (26 test sequences), creating an inherent confounding between camera source and activity classification.
    \item \textbf{Automatic Perception Pipeline Coverage Boundaries}: The perception-enhanced BCS pipeline achieved 93.89\% coverage on ScienceDB (with 489 detector and 2 segmentation failures across 8,040 test images), and the Behavior pipeline retained 96.42\% of test sequences. Performance on perception-failed instances remains outside the evaluated sample distribution.
    \item \textbf{Oracle Segmentation Guidance in Re-ID}: Run 6 and all multi-task Re-ID evaluations utilized released dataset ground-truth masks rather than masks generated by an automatic upstream segmentation model. While this establishes an upper-bound benchmark for foreground isolation, it does not evaluate an automated end-to-end deployment pipeline.
    \item \textbf{Joint Component Attribution}: In the single-task comparisons (Runs 4--6), localization, cropping, binary masks, and temporal convolutions were evaluated in combined configurations. The individual marginal contribution of each isolated component was not ablated independently.
    \item \textbf{Capacity Confounding in Modular Architecture}: The modular adapter architecture (E3) introduced 395,904 additional trainable parameters (+3.32\% capacity over E1). Consequently, observed performance differences cannot be attributed purely to architectural routing in isolation from parameter scale.
    \item \textbf{Boundary on Gradient Conflict Causality}: Direct gradient conflict measurements were recorded exclusively during E4 PCGrad training. These diagnostics show that opposing shared-backbone gradient directions occurred regularly during E4 training, but they do not establish that gradient conflict was the sole causal driver of E1 degradation, nor that E1 experienced identical conflict statistics.
    \item \textbf{Point-Estimate Evaluations}: All reported single-task baselines and multi-task models were evaluated from single trained checkpoints. Computational constraints precluded repeated-seed training distributions, confidence intervals, or formal hypothesis testing; reported metric differences represent descriptive point comparisons.
    \item \textbf{Unexecuted External Stress Tests}: Formal evaluation remained restricted to the primary benchmark datasets. Planned external stress tests on uncalibrated datasets (such as Ruchay, Dryad, MmCows, CBVD-5, and BECA) remain unexecuted roadmap items; results do not imply verified cross-domain generalization.
    \item \textbf{Unverified Downstream Transfer of Pose and Viewpoint}: Anatomical pose estimation and viewpoint priors exhibited high failure rates and source shortcut risks during feasibility audits, and their transfer to downstream task datasets was not verified.
    \item \textbf{Persistence of Negative Transfer}: Despite selective improvements from PCGrad, final multi-task models underperformed dedicated single-task references on several primary metrics, indicating that joint multi-task deployment involves deliberate engineering compromises.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}

Several promising research directions emerge directly from the findings and limitations of this thesis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Repeated-Seed Training and Uncertainty Estimation}: Future investigations should conduct multi-seed training runs to compute confidence intervals, variance bounds, and formal statistical tests across single-task and multi-task configurations.
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Automatic Re-ID Segmentation}: Integrating an upstream automated segmentation model (such as fine-tuned SAM or YOLO-seg) to replace oracle masks with predicted masks, evaluating real-world degradation under segmentation error.
    \item \textbf{External Multi-Herd Generalization Testing}: Evaluating trained models on independent external datasets, including Ruchay2026 and Dryad for BCS, and MmCows or CBVD-5 for behavior recognition, to establish cross-farm generalization.
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Identity Retrieval}: Stress-testing cow re-identification across longitudinal cohorts (such as BECA-L and BECA-D) to assess biometric stability across lactation stages, seasonal coat changes, and physical growth.
    \item \textbf{Isolated Perception Component Ablations}: Conducting factorial ablation studies to isolate the individual contributions of bounding-box localization, binary foreground masking, soft probability masking, and temporal sequence length.
    \item \textbf{Robust Cattle Pose Estimation}: Fine-tuning animal pose models specifically on recumbent, lying, and rear-view cattle postures to overcome the keypoint missingness that currently prevents pose integration into livestock behavior monitoring.
    \item \textbf{Advanced Multi-Task Optimization Strategies}: Investigating partial parameter sharing (such as branched trunks) and adaptive loss-weighting algorithms (such as GradNorm~\cite{chen2018gradnorm}) to further mitigate cross-task gradient tension.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis established an empirical and methodological framework for multi-task deep learning in precision cattle monitoring, evaluating body condition scoring, behavior recognition, and cow re-identification under controlled single-task and joint multi-task settings.

The empirical evidence demonstrates that cattle-centered visual representations provide substantial, measurable utility for individual livestock monitoring tasks, particularly by improving BCS error tolerance and enhancing cow re-identification under unconstrained camera shifts. However, these benefits are task-dependent and do not eliminate the distinct representational requirements inherent to morphology regression, temporal sequence modeling, and biometric identity matching.

When integrated into a unified multi-task framework, naive hard parameter sharing resulted in clear outcome-level negative transfer across all three tasks. Incorporating task-private residual adapters failed to resolve this degradation, while gradient-projected optimization (PCGrad) directly revealed that opposing shared-backbone gradient directions occurred regularly during training. By resolving opposing gradient components, PCGrad provided selective mitigation---notably boosting behavior recognition metrics and authentic barn surveillance performance---yet dedicated single-task models remained superior on several task-specific measures.

Ultimately, these findings demonstrate that multi-task learning in agricultural computer vision does not provide a universal performance benefit through shared representations; successful deployment requires balancing shared cattle features with task-specific capacity and conflict-aware optimization.
