\section{Performance Evaluation}

This chapter reports the six completed single-task experiments. The comparisons are organized by task so that each cattle-centered configuration is evaluated against its corresponding RGB reference. Runs 7 and 8 are not included because the multi-task experiments have not yet been executed. Consequently, the chapter supports task-specific conclusions but not a final claim about the integrated framework.

\subsection{Body Condition Scoring Results}

Run 1 provides the full-test RGB reference on all 8,040 ScienceDB test images. It achieved a physical MAE of 0.1848 BCS units and Acc@1 of 86.74\%. Exact accuracy was 41.63\%, while balanced accuracy and Macro-F1 were 40.41\% and 0.4110. These values show that many errors remained close to the correct ordered class, but exact class separation was considerably harder than prediction within $\pm0.25$ BCS units. The result is a single run over a narrow five-score range and is not a biological cow-disjoint estimate.\evidence{E08}

Run 4 could be evaluated only where the automatic perception pipeline produced both a detection and a non-empty segmentation. Of 8,040 test images, RT-DETR-L succeeded on 7,551 and the complete detector--SAM pipeline succeeded on 7,549. The resulting coverage was 93.89\%, with 489 detector failures and two additional SAM failures. To avoid attributing differences to the filtered sample composition, the Run 1 checkpoint was re-evaluated on exactly the same 7,549 images.\evidence{E24}

\input{tables/bcs_comparison}

On this matched subset, Run 4 reduced MAE from 0.1929 to 0.1709 and increased Acc@1 from 84.95\% to 89.40\%. Exact accuracy also increased from 40.84\% to 43.57\%, while test loss fell from 0.8224 to 0.4403. These are practically important improvements for the principal ordinal measures. However, balanced accuracy decreased from 40.23\% to 39.70\%, and Macro-F1 decreased from 0.4074 to 0.4039. The cattle-centered model therefore improved average score proximity and tolerance accuracy without improving every class-balanced measure.

The matched comparison supports the conclusion that the combined localized RGB crop and binary-mask representation was more effective for the main BCS error criteria on perception-successful images. It does not show that segmentation alone caused the change, because localization, cropping, and the mask channel were introduced together. It also does not describe the 6.11\% of test images for which a complete perception input was unavailable.

\subsection{Behavior Recognition Results}

The full Run 2 single-frame RGB baseline achieved 88.88\% accuracy, 71.72\% balanced accuracy, and 0.7413 Macro-F1 over the 809 canonical test samples. The difference between overall and balanced accuracy reflects unequal class performance. Walking was the clearest weakness: only five of 26 observations were classified correctly, and its F1-score was 0.2174. By comparison, Lying and Feeding obtained F1-scores of 0.9552 and 0.9225.\evidence{E09}

Figure~\ref{fig:behaviorrecall} makes the class imbalance in the RGB baseline visible: Feeding and Lying recall were high, while Walking recall was below 0.20.

The Run 5 perception pipeline retained 780 of the 809 test sequences, giving approximately 96.4\% coverage. The matched comparison therefore re-evaluates Run 2 on those same 780 samples. Table~\ref{tab:behaviorcomparison} distinguishes the historical full baseline from this matched control.

Relative to the matched Run 2 control, Run 5 increased balanced accuracy from 71.30\% to 74.43\% and reduced loss from 0.5505 to 0.4430. Macro-F1 increased slightly from 0.7378 to 0.7397. Overall accuracy, however, decreased from 88.46\% to 87.44\%. The combined cattle-centered temporal configuration therefore redistributed performance rather than producing a uniform gain across all measures.\evidence{E27}

\input{tables/behavior_class_comparison}

The class-wise results clarify this trade-off. Feeding, Drinking, and Walking F1 improved by 0.0247, 0.0252, and 0.0282 respectively. Walking recall rose from 0.1923 to 0.2692, although its final F1 of 0.2456 remained low. Standing and Lying F1 declined by 0.0341 and 0.0343. Thus, temporal foreground-guided processing helped selected activities and the minority Walking class, but it did not preserve the strongest baseline performance for every posture-dominated class.

\input{tables/behavior_source_comparison}

Source-specific results show an equally important difference. Beef accuracy increased from 93.58\% to 96.09\%, with Macro-F1 rising from 0.9079 to 0.9414. CVB accuracy decreased from 84.12\% to 80.09\%, and Macro-F1 fell from 0.6541 to 0.6188. The pooled balanced-accuracy gain is therefore consistent with improved performance on selected classes and on Beef, but not with uniform cross-source improvement. Because Run 5 changes both representation and temporal aggregation, these outcomes cannot be attributed independently to SAM guidance or to the Conv1D module.

\subsection{Cattle Re-Identification Results}

Runs 3 and 6 were evaluated under SideView Protocol A. The 41 cows used for representation learning are disjoint from the 69 evaluation cows. The gallery is always the fixed-camera parlor set; Barn and Snapshot queries are reported separately because their acquisition conditions differ. This protocol evaluates retrieval of unseen identities rather than classification among the 41 training cows.\evidence{E22,E29}

\input{tables/reid_barn_results}

For Barn-to-Parlor retrieval, Run 6 increased Rank-1 from 58.64\% to 63.90\% and mAP from 38.32\% to 40.68\%. Rank-5 and Rank-10 changed from 78.19\% to 77.10\% and from 83.72\% to 82.58\%, respectively. The oracle cattle-centered representation therefore improved the first-match and ranking-quality measures but did not improve every cutoff in the Barn condition.

\input{tables/reid_snapshot_results}

The Snapshot-to-Parlor result was substantially stronger. Rank-1 increased from 38.88\% to 62.93\%, Rank-5 from 57.17\% to 75.29\%, Rank-10 from 64.58\% to 81.05\%, and mAP from 27.05\% to 40.42\%. The 24.05 percentage-point Rank-1 gain was the largest completed improvement in Runs 1--6. It suggests that reliable target-centered geometry and mask guidance were particularly useful under the larger posture, viewpoint, and environment changes represented by snapshot queries.

These results do not establish that automatic segmentation would reproduce the same benefit. Run 6 uses released ground-truth masks to determine the crop and provide the fourth channel. It also changes crop geometry and mask guidance together. The correct conclusion is therefore that the oracle GT segmentation-guided configuration improved cross-setting retrieval, especially for snapshots; shortcut learning was not proven to be eliminated, and the crop and mask effects were not separately identified.

Perception coverage is part of the result because an enhanced model cannot make the same input-level claim for excluded cases. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} reports the retained samples for the completed comparisons.

The BCS result applies directly to 93.89\% of the canonical test set. The Behavior test comparison covers 96.42\% of its canonical samples, with 29 sequences excluded by the perception/occlusion policy. SideView supplies a paired GT mask for every one of its 80,260 images, allowing Runs 3 and 6 to use the full defined Protocol A gallery and query sets; this is dataset-provided oracle coverage, not evidence of automatic mask availability in deployment.

The separate real-cattle viewpoint classifier achieved 86.26\% accuracy, 84.96\% balanced accuracy, and 0.8573 Macro-F1 on 131 held-out images. This establishes that the three-class front/side/rear task was feasible on its own dataset. It was not inserted into Runs 4--6 because cross-domain transfer to ScienceDB, CVB/Beef, and SideView was not validated. The result also cannot be attributed to MOO synthetic pretraining without a same-split ImageNet-initialized control.\evidence{E31}

\section{Analysis of Design Solutions}

The six runs support three different conclusions about cattle-centered representation design. For BCS, the automatic pipeline improved the main ordinal measures on matched perception-successful images, but class-balanced measures were essentially unchanged or slightly worse. For Behavior, the combined four-channel temporal model improved balanced accuracy, loss, Walking, Feeding, Drinking, and Beef performance, while reducing overall accuracy and several CVB/posture measures. For Re-ID, the oracle representation produced a large improvement under the more severe Snapshot-to-Parlor shift and a smaller, mixed change for Barn-to-Parlor retrieval.

\input{tables/design_decisions}

These outcomes argue against treating all three tasks as if they require the same visual representation. BCS benefited mainly in ordered error and tolerance accuracy. Behavior required temporal aggregation but remained sensitive to source and class composition. Re-ID benefited most when the query differed strongly from the controlled gallery. A final shared system should therefore preserve common low-level cattle information while allowing task-specific processing where the evidence requirements diverge. This is a design implication for Runs 7 and 8, not a completed MTL result.

The comparisons also illustrate why perception quality and downstream utility must be separated. High mask overlap in the feasibility audit supported the use of SAM-derived foregrounds, but only the task experiments measured prediction consequences. Conversely, the Run 4 and Run 5 coverage gaps show that useful downstream performance does not remove the operational cost of perception failures.

\section{Final Design Adjustments}

Several design decisions were fixed before the final MTL stage. ScienceDB uses repaired burst groups rather than unsupported biological identities. CVB plus Beef is the primary Behavior stack, with source/session isolation and explicit recognition that Walking is CVB-only. SideView replaces the inaccessible MultiCamCows2024 as the primary Re-ID benchmark and provides an identity-disjoint cross-setting protocol.

Pose was removed from the deadline downstream configurations because the available rear-view outputs and landmark schema were not sufficiently reliable for the intended BCS role. Viewpoint remains a separately validated capability rather than an untested conditioning signal. For the three enhanced task models, the accepted inputs are binary masks rather than continuous soft masks. These adjustments align the thesis with the executed methods rather than older roadmap wording.

The remaining design comparison is explicit. Run 7 will measure an E1 hard-shared control, and Run 8 will measure an E3 modular/task-private alternative. Until both are executed, the final sharing strategy, negative-transfer result, and integrated-system verdict remain open.

\section{Statistical Analysis}

Runs 1--6 are reported primarily as single-run comparisons. Repeated-seed distributions, confidence intervals, p-values, and a preregistered significance test are not available for these final task comparisons. The reported differences are therefore descriptive point estimates and are not called statistically significant.

The matched designs reduce one important source of confounding. Run 4 and its Run 1 control use exactly the same 7,549 images, while Run 5 and its Run 2 control use exactly the same 780 sequences. This makes the within-task metric differences directly comparable on the retained samples. It does not quantify training-seed variability or population uncertainty, and correlated images must not be treated as independent biological cows.

For Re-ID, both models use the same identity-disjoint Protocol A gallery and query sets. The large Snapshot-to-Parlor differences are therefore observed under a common retrieval protocol, but they still arise from one trained model per configuration. Repeated training and group-aware uncertainty estimation remain desirable future additions after the final MTL experiments.

\section{Comparisons and Relationships}

The three tasks use metrics with different meanings and directions. Lower BCS MAE is better, whereas higher Behavior balanced accuracy and Re-ID retrieval scores are better. A single average across these raw metrics would be uninterpretable. The final negative-transfer analysis must compare each MTL task output with its matched single-task reference in that task's own units.

The data protocols also bound the generalization claims. ScienceDB evaluates new burst groups, not verified new cows. CVB plus Beef evaluates protected videos and sessions, not unseen biological identities. SideView Protocol A does evaluate identities excluded from representation learning. These distinctions explain why the Re-ID conclusions can refer to unseen cows while the BCS and primary Behavior conclusions cannot.

External datasets remain planned stress tests rather than completed results. Ruchay and Dryad may extend BCS to different score supports or modalities; MmCows and CBVD-5 may test Behavior transfer; and BECA-L/BECA-D may test longitudinal or population-scale Re-ID. Their presence in the dataset plan is not treated as evidence that the final models generalize to those sources.

\section{Discussions}

The completed BCS comparison is consistent with the cattle-centered hypothesis, but only within a defined boundary. The localized RGB-plus-mask model reduced matched MAE by 0.0220 BCS units and improved Acc@1 by 4.45 percentage points. At the same time, balanced accuracy and Macro-F1 did not improve, and 491 test images lacked a complete perception input. The representation therefore improved proximity to the ordered target more clearly than it improved equal treatment of all five classes.

The Behavior comparison provides a useful negative as well as positive result. Balanced accuracy and loss improved, and Walking, Feeding, Drinking, and Beef metrics moved in a favorable direction. Overall accuracy, CVB Macro-F1, Standing F1, and Lying F1 declined. One plausible interpretation is that the temporal foreground-guided representation reallocates capacity toward changing activities and the more controlled Beef clips, but the experiment does not isolate that mechanism. The result supports further task-specific refinement rather than a claim of universal superiority.

The Re-ID comparison provides the strongest evidence that target-centered visual information can improve robustness under acquisition shift. The gain was much larger for unconstrained snapshots than for barn video, suggesting that the oracle crop-and-mask representation was most helpful when posture, framing, and environment changed together. Because the masks are ground truth, however, Run 6 is best understood as an upper-bound representation study. Deployment would still require an automatic method whose failures and domain transfer are evaluated separately.

Taken together, Runs 1--6 show that cattle-centered representations can improve important outcomes, but the pattern of benefits differs by task and metric. This task dependence is precisely why the final multi-task comparison is necessary. The evidence does not yet establish whether hard sharing causes negative transfer in Phase 3, whether the modular E3 design mitigates it, or whether either MTL model is preferable to the single-task references. Those questions remain the sole major scientific gap before the final integrated discussion and conclusion.
