\section{Performance Evaluation}

This chapter reports the completed single-task and multi-task evaluations, including the monolithic hard-shared control, modular task-private architecture, and PCGrad optimization control. The single-task evaluations first assess whether task-specific cattle-centered representations provide empirical utility over generic RGB baselines on matched populations. The subsequent multi-task evaluations directly examine the central questions of joint training: whether a unified spatial representation induces negative transfer, whether architectural modularity mitigates cross-task degradation, whether gradient projection resolves shared-backbone conflicts, and what training diagnostics reveal about gradient interference during optimization.

\subsection{Body Condition Scoring Results}

Run 1 provides the full-test RGB reference on all 8,040 ScienceDB test images. It achieved a physical MAE of 0.1848 BCS units and Acc@1 of 86.74\%. Exact accuracy was 41.63\%, while balanced accuracy and Macro-F1 were 40.41\% and 0.4110. These values show that many errors remained close to the correct ordered class, but exact class separation was considerably harder than prediction within $\pm0.25$ BCS units. The result is a single run over a narrow five-score range and is not a biological cow-disjoint estimate.\evidence{E08}

Run 4 could be evaluated only where the automatic perception pipeline produced both a detection and a non-empty segmentation. Of 8,040 test images, RT-DETR-L succeeded on 7,551 and the complete detector--SAM pipeline succeeded on 7,549. The resulting coverage was 93.89\%, with 489 detector failures and two additional SAM failures. To avoid attributing differences to the filtered sample composition, the Run 1 checkpoint was re-evaluated on exactly the same 7,549 images.\evidence{E24}

\input{tables/bcs_comparison}

On this matched subset, Run 4 reduced MAE from 0.1929 to 0.1709 and increased Acc@1 from 84.95\% to 89.40\%. Exact accuracy also increased from 40.84\% to 43.57\%, while test loss fell from 0.8224 to 0.4403. These are practically important improvements for the principal ordinal measures. However, balanced accuracy decreased from 40.23\% to 39.70\%, and Macro-F1 decreased from 0.4074 to 0.4039. The cattle-centered model therefore improved average score proximity and tolerance accuracy without improving every class-balanced measure.

The matched comparison supports the conclusion that the combined localized RGB crop and binary-mask representation was more effective for the main BCS error criteria on perception-successful images. It does not show that segmentation alone caused the change, because localization, cropping, and the mask channel were introduced together. It also does not describe the 6.11\% of test images for which a complete perception input was unavailable.

\subsection{Behavior Recognition Results}

The full Run 2 single-frame RGB baseline achieved 88.88\% accuracy, 71.72\% balanced accuracy, and 0.7413 Macro-F1 over the 809 canonical test samples. The difference between overall and balanced accuracy reflects unequal class performance. Walking was the clearest weakness: only five of 26 observations were classified correctly, and its F1-score was 0.2174. By comparison, Lying and Feeding obtained F1-scores of 0.9552 and 0.9225.\evidence{E09}

Figure~\ref{fig:behaviorrecall} makes the class imbalance in the RGB baseline visible: Feeding and Lying recall were high, while Walking recall was below 0.20.

The Run 5 perception pipeline retained 780 of the 809 test sequences, giving approximately 96.4\% coverage. The matched comparison therefore re-evaluates Run 2 on those same 780 samples. Table~\ref{tab:behaviorcomparison} distinguishes the historical full baseline from this matched control.

Relative to the matched Run 2 control, Run 5 increased balanced accuracy from 71.30\% to 74.43\% and reduced loss from 0.5505 to 0.4430. Macro-F1 increased slightly from 0.7378 to 0.7397. Overall accuracy, however, decreased from 88.46\% to 87.44\%. The combined cattle-centered temporal configuration therefore redistributed performance rather than producing a uniform gain across all measures.\evidence{E27}

The class-wise results clarify this trade-off. Feeding, Drinking, and Walking F1 improved by 0.0247, 0.0252, and 0.0282 respectively. Walking recall rose from 0.1923 to 0.2692, although its final F1 of 0.2456 remained low. Standing and Lying F1 declined by 0.0341 and 0.0343. Thus, temporal foreground-guided processing helped selected activities and the minority Walking class, but it did not preserve the strongest baseline performance for every posture-dominated class.

Source-specific results show an equally important difference. Beef accuracy increased from 93.58\% to 96.09\%, with Macro-F1 rising from 0.9079 to 0.9414. CVB accuracy decreased from 84.12\% to 80.09\%, and Macro-F1 fell from 0.6541 to 0.6188. The pooled balanced-accuracy gain is therefore consistent with improved performance on selected classes and on Beef, but not with uniform cross-source improvement. Because Run 5 changes both representation and temporal aggregation, these outcomes cannot be attributed independently to SAM guidance or to the Conv1D module.

\subsection{Cattle Re-Identification Results}

Runs 3 and 6 were evaluated under SideView Protocol A. The 41 cows used for representation learning are disjoint from the 69 evaluation cows. The gallery is always the fixed-camera parlor set; Barn and Snapshot queries are reported separately because their acquisition conditions differ. This protocol evaluates retrieval of unseen identities rather than classification among the 41 training cows.\evidence{E22,E29}

\input{tables/reid_barn_results}

For Barn-to-Parlor retrieval, Run 6 increased Rank-1 from 58.64\% to 63.90\% and mAP from 38.32\% to 40.68\%. Rank-5 and Rank-10 changed from 78.19\% to 77.10\% and from 83.72\% to 82.58\%, respectively. The oracle cattle-centered representation therefore improved the first-match and ranking-quality measures but did not improve every cutoff in the Barn condition.

\input{tables/reid_snapshot_results}

The Snapshot-to-Parlor result was substantially stronger. Rank-1 increased from 38.88\% to 62.93\%, Rank-5 from 57.17\% to 75.29\%, Rank-10 from 64.58\% to 81.05\%, and mAP from 27.05\% to 40.42\%. The 24.05 percentage-point Rank-1 gain was the largest completed improvement in Runs 1--6. It suggests that reliable target-centered geometry and mask guidance were particularly useful under the larger posture, viewpoint, and environment changes represented by snapshot queries.

These results do not establish that automatic segmentation would reproduce the same benefit. Run 6 uses released ground-truth masks to determine the crop and provide the fourth channel. It also changes crop geometry and mask guidance together. The correct conclusion is therefore that the oracle GT segmentation-guided configuration improved cross-setting retrieval, especially for snapshots; shortcut learning was not proven to be eliminated, and the crop and mask effects were not separately identified.

\section{Multi-Task Learning Results}

To evaluate the empirical consequences of joint optimization across the three cattle monitoring domains, all multi-task configurations were evaluated on the exact matched held-out test populations established for the single-task reference models:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Body Condition Scoring}: evaluated on $N = 7{,}549$ ScienceDB test images under the burst-group-disjoint protocol. As established in Chapter~4, this protocol prevents identical-passage sample leakage but does not represent biological cow-disjoint partitioning.
 \item \textbf{Behavior Recognition}: evaluated on $N = 780$ sequence samples ($T = 8$ frames; $6{,}240$ total frames) across authentic barn surveillance (CVB, $N = 422$) and feedlot camera clips (Kaggle Beef, $N = 358$), with the minority Walking class present exclusively in CVB.
 \item \textbf{Cattle Re-Identification}: evaluated under SideView Protocol A across 69 unseen cattle identities against the fixed $36{,}811$-image parlor gallery, reporting $25{,}260$ barn CCTV queries and $607$ handheld snapshot queries with released SideView target masks matching the oracle single-task reference (Run 6).
\end{itemize}
Across all multi-task experiments, model checkpoint selection was strictly governed by minimizing the predefined composite validation loss objective on the validation set, with held-out test populations remaining untouched until final one-time post-training evaluation.

\subsection{Body Condition Scoring Multi-Task Outcomes}

Table~\ref{tab:mtl_bcs_results} summarizes the performance of the three executed multi-task configurations alongside the matched single-task reference model.

\input{tables/mtl_bcs_results}

The monolithic hard-shared control (E1) degraded the principal held-out BCS metrics relative to the matched single-task reference (Run 4). Physical MAE increased from 0.1709 to 0.1788 (+0.0079 BCS units), Acc@1 decreased from 89.40\% to 88.44\% (-0.96 pp), exact accuracy (Acc@0) fell from 43.57\% to 41.10\% (-2.47 pp), balanced accuracy declined from 39.70\% to 35.70\% (-4.00 pp), and Macro-F1 fell from 0.4039 to 0.3605 (-0.0434). Although E1 test loss decreased from 0.4403 to 0.4175, this lower cross-entropy loss alone cannot be characterized as superior estimation quality given the simultaneous degradation across all physical error and class-balanced metrics.

The modular task-private architecture (E3) worsened held-out BCS performance further across every evaluated metric: MAE increased to 0.1916 (+0.0207 over single-task; +0.0128 over E1), Acc@1 dropped to 86.32\%, Acc@0 dropped to 38.47\%, balanced accuracy dropped to 33.13\%, and Macro-F1 dropped to 0.3291, while test loss rose to 0.4555. Introducing task-private residual bottleneck adapters between the shared backbone and the BCS head failed to shield the ordinal scoring representation from cross-task interference.

The PCGrad optimization control (E4) recovered substantial ground relative to E3: MAE improved from 0.1916 to 0.1828 (-0.0088 error reduction), Acc@1 rose from 86.32\% to 87.84\% (+1.52 pp), Acc@0 rose from 38.47\% to 40.23\% (+1.76 pp), balanced accuracy rose from 33.13\% to 34.97\% (+1.84 pp), Macro-F1 rose from 0.3291 to 0.3516 (+0.0225), and test loss fell from 0.4555 to 0.4181 (-0.0374). Relative to E1, E4 was slightly worse on primary physical and classification measures (MAE +0.0040, Acc@1 -0.60 pp, Macro-F1 -0.0089). Crucially, all three multi-task models remained below the matched single-task reference on primary physical error and class-balanced classification metrics, demonstrating consistent outcome-level degradation for BCS under multi-task sharing.

\subsection{Observed Shared-Backbone Gradient Conflict}

Table~\ref{tab:pcgrad_diagnostics} reports the gradient conflict diagnostics recorded during E4 PCGrad multi-task training across all 30 epochs ($16{,}140$ super-steps).

\input{tables/pcgrad_gradient_diagnostics}

Across the complete 30-epoch training schedule, the PCGrad diagnostics engine recorded 44,177 pairwise conflict projections, averaging 2.737 conflict projections per super-step (out of a theoretical maximum of 6 pairwise evaluations per step). Pairwise conflict frequencies were remarkably consistent across task pairs: BCS and Behavior gradients pointed in opposing directions in 47.8\% of super-steps, BCS and Re-ID in 46.5\%, and Behavior and Re-ID in 47.4\%.

These diagnostics provide direct empirical evidence that opposing gradient directions on the shared four-channel ResNet-18 spatial backbone occurred regularly during multi-task training. In naive hard sharing (E1), these opposing components were directly accumulated, resulting in cancellation during updates. In E4, projecting conflicting components resolved opposing directions prior to parameter updates.

However, strict scientific claim boundaries must be maintained: these measurements do not prove that gradient conflict caused all observed E1 degradation, that E1 experienced identical conflict frequencies, that PCGrad eliminated all conflict, or that the measured conflict proves causality for negative transfer. Rather, they provide descriptive optimization evidence of shared-parameter gradient tension during joint learning.

\section{Analysis of Multi-Task Design Solutions}

The empirical evaluations across single-task and multi-task configurations support distinct findings regarding the three multi-task design hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Naive Hard Parameter Sharing (E1)}: Naively forcing three heterogeneous agricultural vision tasks into a single four-channel ResNet-18 spatial trunk produced held-out degradation across BCS, Behavior, and principal Re-ID retrieval metrics. This outcome-level negative transfer demonstrates that mutual synergy cannot be assumed when combining coarse body shape estimation (BCS), localized temporal motion (Behavior), and fine-grained biometric surface patterns (Re-ID). The specific internal feature corruption, however, cannot be isolated from E1 alone.
 \item \textbf{Modular Task-Private Adapters (E3)}: Introducing task-private residual bottleneck adapters between the shared backbone and each prediction head failed to provide general negative-transfer mitigation. While Behavior test loss and CVB surveillance accuracy improved over E1, BCS and Re-ID degraded further, and Behavior balanced accuracy and Macro-F1 declined. Furthermore, because E3 added 395,904 trainable parameters (+3.32\% capacity), its performance differences cannot be attributed purely to architectural routing.
 \item \textbf{Gradient-Projected Optimization (E4)}: By applying PCGrad projection exclusively to shared backbone gradients, E4 provided a controlled optimization comparison matching E1 parameter-for-parameter (11,926,706 trainable parameters; zero adapters). E4 provided selective, metric-dependent mitigation of E1 negative transfer, notably improving Behavior overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, Macro-F1, test loss, and CVB surveillance performance, while recovering principal Re-ID and BCS metrics relative to E3. However, E4 remained below dedicated single-task models on several primary metrics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Statistical Analysis and Uncertainty Boundaries}

All single-task baselines and multi-task configurations reported in this chapter are evaluated as single trained checkpoints (point estimates). Repeated-seed training distributions, confidence intervals, formal hypothesis tests, and p-values were not computed. Consequently, the reported differences are descriptive point comparisons and are not characterized as statistically significant.

The matched-sample design reduces sample-composition bias: Run 4, E1, E3, and E4 evaluate the exact same 7,549 ScienceDB images; Run 5, E1, E3, and E4 evaluate the exact same 780 Behavior sequences; and Run 6, E1, E3, and E4 evaluate the exact same SideView Protocol A query and gallery populations. This guarantees valid internal comparisons on the retained samples. However, it does not quantify seed variability or population variance, and correlated video frames or bursts must not be treated as independent biological samples.

Similarly, the recorded PCGrad conflict frequencies (Table~\ref{tab:pcgrad_diagnostics}) represent descriptive training diagnostics from a single optimization trajectory, not hypothesis tests. Small metric differences (such as E4 vs. E1 on Re-ID snapshot mAP of $+0.18$ pp) cannot be claimed as statistically equivalent.

\section{Cross-Task Relationships and Claim Boundaries}

The three evaluation domains employ metrics with fundamentally different scales, units, and optimization directions:
\begin{itemize}
 \item BCS uses physical Mean Absolute Error in BCS units (where lower is better), Acc@1 within $\pm 0.25$ units, and exact ordinal accuracy.
 \item Behavior uses overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, Macro-F1 across 5 classes, and per-class F1-scores (where higher is better).
 \item Re-ID uses cumulative match characteristics (Rank-1, Rank-5, Rank-10) and mean Average Precision across open-set retrieval rankings (where higher is better).
\end{itemize}
Because these metrics are incommensurable, computing an aggregate multi-task score across all three tasks would be arbitrary and uninformative. Negative transfer must be evaluated within each task domain against its matched single-task reference.

The evaluation populations also define strict generalization boundaries:
\begin{itemize}
 \item ScienceDB evaluates unseen burst groups from automated passage sequences, not verified unseen biological cows.
 \item Behavior evaluates protected video recordings and sessions, not cow-disjoint herds; Walking is evaluated solely within CVB barn surveillance.
 \item SideView Protocol A evaluates 69 biologically distinct cows excluded from representation learning. However, Run 6 and all multi-task Re-ID evaluations use released dataset ground-truth masks rather than automatic segmentations, representing an oracle condition.
\end{itemize}
External datasets referenced in the broader roadmap (such as Ruchay, Dryad, MmCows, CBVD-5, and BECA) remain unexecuted stress tests; their documentation does not imply cross-domain generalization.

\section{Discussion of Findings}

The empirical findings presented in this chapter establish three main conclusions:

First, cattle-centered visual representations provide clear, measurable benefits on matched single-task populations, but their effectiveness is strongly task-dependent. Localized cropping and binary mask guidance improved BCS physical error proximity (reducing MAE by 0.0220 units and improving Acc@1 by 4.45 pp) without improving class-balanced measures. For Behavior, combining cattle crops, masks, and temporal 1D convolution improved balanced accuracy (+3.13 pp), reduced loss (-19.5\%), and improved minority Walking F1 (+0.0282), while redistributing performance across classes and sources. For Re-ID, target-centered oracle segmentation produced a substantial gain under unconstrained handheld snapshot queries (+24.05 pp Rank-1; +13.37 pp mAP), where environmental and posture variations were greatest.

Second, naively integrating these three tasks under monolithic hard parameter sharing (E1) resulted in consistent outcome-level negative transfer. On matched held-out test populations, E1 degraded BCS MAE (+0.0079), Behavior balanced accuracy (-7.13 pp), Walking F1 (-0.2048), and Re-ID Barn Rank-1 (-6.52 pp) and mAP (-10.31 pp). This confirms that sharing a single four-channel ResNet-18 spatial backbone across tasks with conflicting representational demands induces negative transfer.

Third, the comparative evaluation of modularity (E3) versus gradient projection (E4) demonstrates that resolving multi-task tension is subtle and metric-dependent:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Architectural modularity via task-private residual adapters (E3) improved Behavior test loss and CVB surveillance accuracy over E1, but degraded BCS and Re-ID further, and failed to improve balanced accuracy or Macro-F1. Adding private parameters alone did not resolve shared representation interference.
 \item Optimization-level gradient projection (E4) provided more effective, selective mitigation. E4 achieved the highest Behavior overall accuracy (86.92\%), balanced accuracy (69.15\%), and Macro-F1 (0.7114) among multi-task models, more than doubled Walking F1 relative to E1 (0.0909 vs. 0.0408), and recovered principal BCS and Re-ID metrics relative to E3 while preserving exact parameter equality with E1 (11,926,706 parameters).
 \item Direct optimization diagnostics recorded 44,177 gradient conflict projections during E4 training (averaging 2.737 projections per super-step), empirically verifying regular gradient opposition across task pairs.
\end{itemize}

Nonetheless, dedicated single-task models remained superior on several task-specific metrics, including BCS MAE (0.1709 single-task vs. 0.1828 E4), Behavior balanced accuracy (74.43\% single-task vs. 69.15\% E4), Walking F1 (0.2456 single-task vs. 0.0909 E4), and Re-ID Barn Rank-1 (63.90\% single-task vs. 54.97\% E4). These results refute the assumption that multi-task learning universally benefits individual tasks through shared regularizing representations. Instead, they demonstrate that different cattle monitoring tasks require distinct spatial and temporal feature representations, and that multi-task deployment involves deliberate, metric-dependent engineering trade-offs.
