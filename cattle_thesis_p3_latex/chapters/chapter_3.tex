\section{Final Specifications and Requirements}

The framework is intended to produce a body condition estimate, a behavior label, and an identity representation for cattle retrieval. It is evaluated as a research prototype rather than a deployed farm product. Its requirements therefore cover both prediction and the controls needed to interpret results: defined label spaces, protected data partitions, explicit failure handling, task-appropriate metrics, and reproducible configurations.

\input{tables/requirements}

The BCS pathway must predict within the documented physical score range. The behavior pathway must use the fixed five-class taxonomy. The Re-ID pathway must specify the learning identities, gallery, queries, and retrieval measures. Requirement R4 concerns the integration of all three tasks in a multi-task model, with explicit documentation of shared and task-specific parameters.

The non-functional requirements are reproducibility, leakage resistance, maintainability, transparent failure reporting, and feasible execution. Dataset manifests, scripts, metrics, and configurations are versioned, while raw datasets and large checkpoints are stored separately. Reproduction requires access to both the recorded configuration and the corresponding data.

Scientific validity imposes further requirements. Test data must not guide checkpoint selection. Comparisons affected by perception failures must use matched samples or clearly report coverage. Missing task labels must be handled through task-specific batches or an available-label mechanism, not interpreted as negative labels. Evaluation claims must also match the protected grouping unit: burst group, source video, recording session, or biological identity.

\section{Societal Impact}

An integrated visual system could organize repeated cattle observations by associating body condition and behavior with an individual animal. This may support earlier review of unusual changes and reduce the burden of inspecting recordings manually. These are potential applications, not measured impacts: the study does not include farmer interviews, a deployment trial, or measurements of labor, productivity, treatment outcomes, or animal welfare.

Automated outputs should support human assessment rather than replace it. An identity error could attach observations to the wrong animal, while an incorrect behavior or BCS estimate could delay attention. A practical interface should retain the source image, time, confidence or retrieval evidence, and a means of human review. Veterinary and welfare decisions require appropriate professional judgment.

Accessibility depends on the operating context. Camera placement, housing, breed, lighting, network access, and staff practices vary between farms. Results on the selected datasets cannot establish suitability for every production environment, including farms in Bangladesh. Evaluation with intended users and local conditions would be required before claiming usability or adoption.

Farm imagery may contain people, layouts, or commercially sensitive information. Permission to access a dataset for research does not necessarily permit redistribution or commercial deployment. Privacy, ownership, and licensing conditions must be considered for each source and any future field installation.

\section{Environmental Impact}

The computational footprint includes perception preprocessing, model training, storage, and data transfer, not only the downstream predictor. A compact prediction network can still depend on a comparatively expensive detector or segmentation model. Efficiency claims must therefore state whether upstream processing is included.

We limited repeated computation by validating the pipeline before full experiments and reusing cached perception outputs and deterministic manifests. These practices reduce duplicated processing, but no complete power trace or location-specific emissions measurement was collected. The study therefore reports neither measured emissions nor carbon savings.

Improved monitoring could support resource-efficient livestock management. However, the experiments do not measure feed savings, methane reduction, or changes in disease burden. Such effects would depend on how predictions influence farm decisions and would require a separate longitudinal evaluation.

\section{Ethical Issues}

Responsible reporting requires a clear distinction between evidence and inference. ScienceDB is not described as cow-disjoint because biological identifiers are unavailable, and the primary behavior test is not presented as unseen-cow evaluation. The oracle segmentation-guided Re-ID experiment uses ground-truth masks; it does not demonstrate automatic segmentation. Mixed and negative findings are reported alongside improvements.

Dataset reuse must be considered separately for each source. The dataset registry records available licenses and access conditions, but unresolved permissions must be confirmed before redistribution or deployment. No institutional ethics approval, exemption, user study, or field consent process is claimed without a supporting record.

Model selection uses validation data, while held-out task tests are reserved for evaluation. External test results must not be used to tune the model after inspection. Samples excluded because of perception failures are reported through coverage statistics rather than omitted without explanation.

The authors remain responsible for AI-assisted writing and coding. They must review the manuscript, verify its claims, disclose assistance according to university policy, and confirm the declaration and acknowledgments.

\section{Standards and Technical Conventions}

The study follows documented technical conventions without claiming certification against an unverified engineering standard. BCS labels are interpreted using their released score range and established anatomical context~\cite{edmonson1989bcs,ferguson1994bcs}. Behavior labels use a versioned mapping, and Re-ID uses a defined query--gallery protocol. These conventions support consistent evaluation but do not constitute product certification.

The manuscript follows the supplied BRAC University chapter structure and an IEEE-style bibliography. Dataset licenses, privacy requirements, deployment safety, and any separate IEEE-format submission requirement need independent confirmation. Reference formatting does not imply journal submission or acceptance.

The research is organized around protocol verification, perception assessment, single-task reference models, cattle-centered alternatives, and multi-task comparison. \section{Risk Management}

Table~\ref{tab:risks} summarizes the principal scientific and operational risks. These include correlated observations crossing partitions, unsupported identity claims, source-linked classes, perception failures, causal attribution from combined configurations, and unequal training budgets in multi-task comparisons.

\input{tables/risks}

The evaluation design addresses these risks through task-specific controls. ScienceDB burst groups and behavior videos or sessions remain within their assigned partitions, while SideView Protocol A separates biological identities between learning and evaluation. Matched subsets support comparison of the RGB and perception-enhanced BCS and behavior models on the same retained samples. The Re-ID model's oracle designation distinguishes ground-truth guidance from an automatic pipeline. Important limitations remain: ScienceDB identities are unknown, Walking is source-confounded, and the task models provide single-run estimates. The final sharing comparison must retain equivalent task protocols and disclose any differences in training budget.
