\section{Final Specifications and Requirements}

The intended outcome is a research framework that produces three outputs: a body condition estimate, a behavior label, and an identity representation suitable for cattle retrieval. The system is evaluated as an experimental prototype rather than as a deployed farm product. Its requirements therefore include not only predictive functions, but also the controls needed to interpret those predictions: named label spaces, protected data partitions, explicit failure handling, task-appropriate metrics, and reproducible configurations.

\input{tables/requirements}

Functionally, the BCS pathway must predict within the documented physical score support; the Behavior pathway must use the fixed five-class taxonomy; and the Re-ID pathway must define the identities, gallery, queries, and retrieval metric. The eventual multi-task model must expose all three outputs while documenting which parameters are shared and which remain task-specific. Since Runs 7 and 8 are pending, requirement R4 is not yet satisfied.

The principal non-functional requirements are reproducibility, leakage resistance, maintainability, transparent failure reporting, and feasible execution. Small manifests, scripts, metrics, and configuration records are versioned, whereas raw datasets and large checkpoints remain in persistent data storage. This separation requires an environment-level readiness check before execution; a manifest alone does not prove that all referenced files are present.

Scientific validity imposes additional requirements. Test data must not guide checkpoint selection. Comparisons involving perception failures must use matched samples or report coverage clearly. Missing task labels in the final MTL setting must be masked or handled through task-specific batches rather than interpreted as negative labels. Finally, a result may be generalized only to the split unit actually protected: a burst group, source video, recording session, or biological identity.

\section{Societal Impact}

An integrated visual system could help organize repeated observations of cattle by associating body condition and behavior with an individual animal. In principle, this may support earlier review of unusual changes and reduce the burden of manually inspecting every recording. The thesis does not, however, contain farmer interviews, a deployment trial, or measured changes in labor, productivity, treatment outcome, or animal welfare. These remain potential applications rather than demonstrated impacts.

Automated outputs should assist rather than replace responsible human assessment. A false identity match could attach observations to the wrong animal, while an incorrect behavior or BCS estimate could delay attention. A practical interface should therefore retain the source image, time, confidence or retrieval evidence, and a route for human review. Veterinary or welfare decisions should not be made from a model output without appropriate professional judgment.

Accessibility is also context dependent. Camera placement, housing style, breed, lighting, network access, and staff practices differ among farms. Performance on the selected datasets cannot establish suitability for every production environment, including farms in Bangladesh. Evaluation with intended users and local operating conditions would be required before claiming usability or adoption.

Images may contain people, farm layouts, or commercially sensitive information. Dataset access and research use do not automatically imply unrestricted redistribution or commercial deployment. Applicable privacy, ownership, and licensing requirements must be reviewed for each source and for any future field installation.

\section{Environmental Impact}

The environmental cost of the system includes perception preprocessing, model training, storage, and transfer, not only the final downstream network. A compact ResNet-18 head can still depend on a comparatively expensive detector or segmentation model. Claims about efficiency must therefore define whether upstream processing is included.

The project limits avoidable computation by using smoke tests before full runs, caching validated perception outputs, reusing deterministic manifests, and assigning network-only jobs minimal cloud resources. Local hardware is reserved for small validation tasks, while full preprocessing and training use cloud accelerators. These practices reduce duplicated work, but no complete power trace or location-specific emissions measurement was collected. The thesis therefore does not report carbon savings or measured emissions.

Improved monitoring could eventually support resource-efficient livestock management, but the completed experiments do not quantify feed savings, methane reduction, or changes in disease burden. Such outcomes depend on how predictions alter real farm decisions and would require a separate longitudinal evaluation.

\section{Ethical Issues}

The principal ethical obligation in this work is to preserve the boundary between observed evidence and unsupported inference. ScienceDB is not described as cow-disjoint because biological IDs are unavailable. The primary Behavior test is not described as unseen-cow evaluation. Run 6 is identified as an oracle ground-truth-mask experiment rather than evidence of automatic segmentation. Mixed and negative findings are reported alongside improvements.

Dataset reuse must be assessed separately for each source. Existing registry information records available licenses and access conditions, but unresolved permissions must be confirmed before redistribution or deployment. No institutional ethics approval, exemption, user study, or field consent process is claimed unless supported by an institutional record.

Test integrity is protected by using validation data for model selection and holding task test sets for post-training evaluation. External datasets must not be used opportunistically for tuning after their results are observed. Similarly, exclusions caused by perception are reported as coverage rather than hidden from the comparison.

AI-assisted writing and coding do not transfer responsibility away from the authors. The final team must review the manuscript, verify all claims, describe assistance according to university policy, and confirm the final declaration and acknowledgments.

\section{Standards -- if applicable}

The project follows documented technical conventions rather than claiming certification against an unverified engineering standard. BCS labels are interpreted through their released score support and established anatomical context~\cite{edmonson1989bcs,ferguson1994bcs}. Behavior labels follow a versioned mapping, and Re-ID follows a named query--gallery protocol. These controls improve consistency but are not formal product certification.

The manuscript follows the supplied BRAC University chapter structure and uses an IEEE-style bibliography. Dataset licenses, privacy requirements, deployment safety, and any separate IEEE-format deliverable require independent confirmation. Bibliographic formatting is not evidence of journal submission or acceptance.

Resource allocation follows the compute policy of the repository. The local GTX 1050 Ti is used only for smoke tests, path checks, tensor/loss/metric verification, and checkpoint save/reload tests. Heavy preprocessing and full training are dispatched to Modal cloud GPUs, generally beginning with the lowest suitable tier. The BRACU RTX 5090 machine is disqualified because its software environment is unusable and was not used for the reported Phase 3 training.

These controls reduce operational risk when cloud profiles rotate, while the thesis reports the scientific method independently of any one account name.

Team contribution, final author order, committee details, and submission metadata remain administrative matters for confirmation by the authors and supervisors. They are not inferred from Git authorship or generated automatically in this manuscript.

The most consequential are correlated observations crossing partitions, overstated identity claims, source-linked classes, perception failures, causal claims from combined configurations, and an MTL comparison whose training budget is not matched.

The completed experiments address these risks in different ways. Grouped manifests protect ScienceDB bursts and Behavior videos/sessions, while SideView protects biological identities for Protocol A. Matched subsets make Runs 1/4 and Runs 2/5 directly comparable on perception-successful samples. Run 6's oracle status prevents the GT masks from being confused with automatic deployment. Residual risks remain: ScienceDB identity is unknown, Walking is source-confounded, the task models are single-run estimates, and the final sharing comparison has not yet been performed.
