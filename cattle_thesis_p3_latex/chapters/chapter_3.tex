\section{Final Specifications and Requirements}

The framework is meant to give three results: an estimate of the animal's body condition, a label for its behavior, and a representation of its identity for retrieval. As a research prototype, not as a finished farm product, it is being looked at. So, the requirements include both functions that make predictions and the controls that make sure the results are properly interpreted. Some of these are clear label definitions, protected data splits, failure handling, task-specific metrics, and settings that allow the results to be reproduced.

\input{tables/requirements}

For BCS, the model must predict within the documented score range. The behavior model must use the fixed five-class label set. The Re-ID model must clearly define the learning identities, gallery, queries, and retrieval metrics. Requirement R4 covers the final integration of all three tasks into one multi-task model, including a clear description of what is shared and what remains task-specific.

The main non-functional requirements are reproducibility, leakage resistance, maintainability, clear failure reporting, and practical execution. Dataset manifests, scripts, metrics, and configurations are versioned. Raw datasets and large checkpoints are stored separately. Reproducing an experiment therefore requires both the recorded configuration and access to the correct data.

Scientific validity adds further requirements. Test data must not be used for checkpoint selection. If perception failures remove samples, comparisons must either use matched samples or report the coverage clearly. Missing task labels must be handled with task-specific batches or another available-label method, not treated as negative labels. Finally, every evaluation claim must match the unit protected by the split, such as a burst group, source video, recording session, or biological identity.

\section{Societal Impact}

A common visual monitoring system may be useful to organize repeated observation of cattle by associating body condition and behavior to individual animals. This can help people see unusual changes earlier, and reduce the amount of manual video inspection that has to be done in practice. However, this study does not incorporate farmer interviews, field deployment or evaluation of labor savings, productivity, treatment outcomes or benefits in animal welfare. Those are prospective benefits, not measured results.

The model should assist human decision making, not replace it. Poor identification matching can lead to observations being assigned to the wrong cow, and an inaccurate BCS or behavior forecast can cause to a delay in attention. A realistic system has to save the original image, the time, the confidence or proof of retrieval and a way for a person to judge the output. There still has to be good professional judgment in veterinary and welfare decisions.

The accessiblity of the monitoring system also depends on the farm environment. Placement of cameras, housing, breed, lighting, network access, and how staff work can be very different. Based on the results from the chosen datasets, it is not possible to say for sure that the system will work in all production settings, such as Bangladeshi farms. Before claiming that something will be used or adopted in the real world, it should be tested with people in that area and under their own conditions.

Agricultural pictures can also show people, arrangements, or private data. It's not always included in the license to use a dataset for research that it can be shared or used for business. Privacy, ownership, and license rules need to be checked for each collection and each possible field use.

\section{Environmental Impact}

Perception preprocessing, training models, storing data, and sending data are all parts of the system's computational cost. It is not limited to the final prediction network. A bigger detector or segmentation model can affect even a small model further down the line. We need to make it clear if upstream processing is part of any efficiency claim because of this.

We reduced repeated computation by checking the pipeline before full experiments, reusing validated cached outputs, and using deterministic manifests. These steps reduce unnecessary repeated processing, but we did not collect a complete power trace or location-specific emissions measurement. Therefore, the study does not claim measured carbon savings or measured emissions.

Better monitoring could eventually support more efficient livestock management. However, the experiments do not measure feed savings, methane reduction, or changes in disease burden. Those effects would depend on how the predictions change real farm decisions and would need a separate long-term study.

\section{Ethical Issues}

A major ethical responsibility in this work is to separate what the data actually show from what we might assume. ScienceDB is not described as cow-disjoint because verified biological cow IDs are not available. The primary behavior evaluation is not described as unseen-cow evaluation. The oracle segmentation-guided Re-ID experiment uses ground-truth masks, so it is not evidence of an automatic segmentation system. Positive, mixed, and negative findings are all reported.

Dataset reuse must also be checked for each source. The dataset registry records the available licenses and access conditions, but any unresolved permission must be confirmed before redistribution or deployment. The thesis does not claim institutional ethics approval, an exemption, a user study, or field consent unless a supporting institutional record exists.

The model selection is done using validation data, while the evaluation is done using held-out test data. Do not use external test data for tuning after you have seen the results. Coverage information is used to report samples that are discarded due to perception failures, rather than being silently ignored.

The authors are still in charge of work that was written or coded with help from AI. They have to look over the final draft, make sure the claims are true, follow the university's rules on disclosure, and make sure the statement and acknowledgments are correct.

\section{Standards and Technical Conventions}

The study follows well-known technical rules, but it doesn't claim to be certified by an engineering standard that hasn't been checked. The released score range and known anatomical meaning are used to figure out what the BCS labels mean~\cite{edmonson1989bcs,ferguson1994bcs}. Labels for behavior use a versioned mapping, and Re-ID has a clear query--gallery protocol. That being said, these rules do make the evaluation more fair, but they do not officially certify the method.

The thesis is prepared according to the prescribed chapter format of BRAC University and bibliography style of IEEE. Dataset licensing, privacy requirements, deployment safety and any separate IEEE-format submission requirement need to be reviewed independently. The use of IEEE reference format does not imply that the work has been submitted or approved for publication to the Journal.

Computational resources were selected according to the needs of preprocessing, training, and evaluation. \section{Risk Management}

There is a list of the main scientific and operational risks in Table~\ref{tab:risks}. Some of these are related observations that cross train and test splits, identity claims that can't be backed up, classes that are linked to the same data source, perception failures, wrong causal claims from combined model changes, and unfair comparisons caused by different training budgets.

\input{tables/risks}

Task-specific controls in the evaluation design lower these risks. ScienceDB burst groups and behavior videos or sessions stay in the partitions that were given to them. SideView Protocol A separates biological identities between representation learning and evaluation. We use matched subsets to check how the RGB, perception-enhanced BCS, and behavior models work on the same samples that were kept. When ground-truth masks are used, the Re-ID model is clearly called "oracle-guided." There are still some important limits: Individual cow identities in ScienceDB are unknown, Walking is linked to a single source, and the task models are based on a single run. The final MTL comparison needs to keep the task protocols comparable and report any changes in the training budget.
